# Shipd — Copy Voice

## Voice

- Founder explaining a real problem, not a brand announcing a feature.
- Conversational, like talking to someone, not presenting to them.
- Personality level around 8/10. Opinionated, not neutral.
- Plain vocabulary over impressive vocabulary.
- Comfortable admitting uncertainty instead of pretending to have every answer.

## Writing Style

- Start with an observation, a story, a real moment, or a real number. A stat or a cited fact can open an argument just as well as a story.
- Introduce the problem before the idea.
- Explain why the problem matters before naming the solution.
- Bring in comparisons or examples to build the argument.
- Sometimes challenge the argument before landing on it. Ask "but does it matter" before answering it.
- Willing to call out a specific weakness directly instead of staying diplomatic about it. State the flaw, then explain it.
- Arrive at the idea last, once the reader understands why it matters.
- Close by pointing back at responsibility. Who needs to act, and what happens if they do not.
- No unnecessary hyphens.
- No exclamation marks.
- No emojis.

## What It Should Sound Like

- A founder making a strong argument.
- A product designer with a sharp point of view.
- Someone who has actually thought about the problem, not someone selling a solution to it.
- Natural rhythm can include phrases like "Here's the thing," "The problem is," "You could argue," "I mean" — used where they fit, never forced in.

## Avoid

- seamless, robust, empower, unlock, supercharge
- "Launch your…", "Unlock…", "Supercharge…", "The future of…"
- Corporate marketing tone, generic SaaS tone, AI-generated tone, Web3 protocol-announcement tone.
- Clever taglines that skip the explanation. A line that sounds good but doesn't teach the reader anything is a rejected line.
- Trying too hard to sound smart or impressive.

## Core Principle

Tell the story. Explain the problem. Make the argument. Then present the idea. Never write the conclusion before the reader has a reason to care about it.

## Rule

If the copy could appear on any other startup's landing page, it is wrong for Shipd.
